02
CREDIBILITY

Pro judges for Shark Tank + TechStart. No more student panels.

Right now, fellows pitch to other fellows. The feedback is friendly. The signal is weak.

I'd swap student judges for working VCs, operators, and founders — people whose feedback actually counts, and whose follow-ups can turn into checks. Pitching to a real partner once is worth more than ten student rounds.

2–3 working VCs per major pitch event (NYC + remote)
1 active founder at every panel for product feedback
Office-hours follow-ups for top teams — judges become mentors
Recorded feedback shared with all fellows, not just the finalists
03
ECOSYSTEM

Tap NYC. It's 40 minutes away.

RSVP is "Road to Silicon V/Alley." We're already on the V/Alley side of the slash. Let's act like it.

NYC has the second-densest concentration of seed-stage VCs and tech operators in the country. We don't use it. I'd run monthly NYC days — coffee chats, founder office hours, fund visits, and informal demo nights — so fellows are pitching in a real ecosystem before they even graduate.

Monthly NYC trip — train down, 3–4 stops, back by dinner
Demo nights hosted at NYC accelerator + VC offices
RSVP × NYC operator meetups — open invite to alumni who work in the city
04
DEPTH

Long-term mentorship for actual founders.

One-off speaker events disappear from your brain in a week. Year-long mentor relationships change your trajectory.

I'd launch a structured mentorship program matching fellows running real startups with operators, founders, and VCs for the full academic year. Monthly check-ins. Goal-tracking. Real accountability. The mentors I've already met through Google, NASA, and Columbia are willing — they just need someone to set up the program.

1:1 matching for serious founders — application-based
Monthly cadence — 30 min calls, structured check-in template
Mentor pool drawn from Google / Goldman / NASA / Columbia / a16z / YC alums
Year-end demo — mentors come to see what their founders shipped
05
PROGRAMMING

Panels > one-off speakers.

A single speaker event = one perspective. A panel = three operators disagreeing, which is how you actually learn.

I'd shift the speaker format toward 3-person panels with a real moderator, organized by theme: AI infra, healthtech, fintech, consumer, climate, frontier robotics. Each panel becomes a recruiting event — alums and partners want to be in the room when the conversation is good.

1 themed panel/month — operator + investor + technical-founder
Pre-read sent to fellows — show up sharp, not blank
Recorded + clipped — content that recruits next year's cohort
06
CULTURE

Social events. Board bonding. Make the family real.

The thing I keep coming back to in my own RSVP story: I didn't stay because of the curriculum. I stayed because of the people.

Programming alone doesn't build a cohort that goes on to co-found companies together. Time outside the curriculum does. I'd run regular cohort socials, board retreats, and informal off-campus dinners — the kind of thing that turns a fellowship into a network you'll have for ten years.

Monthly cohort socials — low-lift, low-cost, high-frequency
Board retreat at the start of each semester — alignment + bonding
Fellowship dinners — small groups, off-campus, real conversation
Alumni weekend once a year — bring back Cohorts 1–6 to mentor 7+8
